# Story

A motion ad is a story in four or five beats, not four or five slogans. Pick one arc, give each beat one job, and let the last beat hold.

## From a brief

When a brief exists (from the positioning-brief skill or from the user), take its promise, audience, proof, do-not-claim list, and arc. Map the arc onto beats with the table below. When it names no framework, choose one by goal.

## Arcs in four or five beats

| Framework | Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4 | End card (holds) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic narrative | The shift in the world | Who wins and who is left behind | The promised land | How the offer gets you there | Proof and the ask |
| PAS | The problem, in the customer's words | What it costs | The fix | | The ask |
| BAB | Before | After | The offer as the bridge | | The ask |
| ABT | And: the setup | But: the turn | Therefore: the offer | | The ask |
| AIDA | Attention | Interest | Desire, with proof | | Action |

The scaffold's beats (`hook`, `friction`, `change`, `end`) already follow a four-beat arc. Rename and re-time them to the arc you chose.

## Rules for the arc

- One job per beat. If a beat can go without breaking the story, cut it.
- The turn is the strongest moment. Give it the most room and the biggest visual change.
- Proof is a real number, a real name, or a checkable fact. Without one, leave the proof out rather than soften it.
- The end card answers "what do I do now?" with one ask.
- The last frame can rhyme with the first: the same object, resolved.
