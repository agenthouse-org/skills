# Worked Example: Superficial Transformation Program

## Situation

A company introduced agile titles, a new project tool, and reorganized reporting lines. Meeting volume increased, decision speed did not improve, and customer lead time remained unchanged.

## Diagnosis

This resembles the book's `f(x) = ax + b` warning: the expression looks different while the result remains the same. The evidence does not yet prove that the transformation failed, but it shows that visible structural change has not changed the measured outcome.

## Intervention

1. State the original causal assumptions behind the program.
2. Compare decision rights, incentives, handoffs, and approval times before and after.
3. Trace one customer request through the new system.
4. Remove one demonstrably redundant approval or handoff in a bounded pilot.
5. Measure lead time and rework rather than tool adoption alone.

## Executive answer

Pause further rollout of labels and tooling until leadership can show how they change decisions and customer flow. Test one causal mechanism in a bounded process and use the result to determine whether to adapt, continue, or stop the program.
