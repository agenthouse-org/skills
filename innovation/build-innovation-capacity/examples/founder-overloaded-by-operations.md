# Worked Example: Founder Overloaded by Operations

## Situation

A founder says the company must find a new growth engine, but spends most weeks approving discounts, resolving delivery escalations, reviewing routine reports, and joining every hiring decision. Product experiments are discussed but rarely started.

## Classification

- Discount approvals, delivery escalations, and routine reporting: RUN.
- Hiring and launching defined experiments: BUILD.
- Exploring a new growth engine and questioning the current offer: ALPHA.

## Diagnosis

The visible problem is lack of innovation time. The likely binding constraint is centralized RUN decision authority. The founder is also an operational dependency, so simply blocking “innovation Fridays” would probably fail.

## Intervention

1. Sample two weeks of founder decisions and group them by role and domain.
2. Delegate bounded discount and delivery decisions through explicit authorities and escalation thresholds.
3. Protect a small weekly ALPHA block only after the new authority model begins operating.
4. Convert one growth idea into a customer hypothesis and five exploratory interviews.

## Validation

- RUN escalations reaching the founder decline without harming margin or service.
- The delegated roles resolve cases within their authority.
- At least one growth assumption gains or loses evidence within three weeks.

## Executive answer

Your immediate innovation constraint is not a shortage of ideas; it is the founder's role as the default operating approval layer. Release decision capacity from RUN first, then use a protected block to test one growth hypothesis rather than opening a broad innovation program.
