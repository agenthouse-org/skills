# Executive Diagnosis

## Diagnostic lenses

### Attention

Estimate where leadership time, employee capacity, budget, and meeting attention go. Use ranges when exact data is unavailable. Ask what repeatedly interrupts planned work.

### Flow

Trace one representative idea from first discussion to reliable operation. Locate waiting, approval loops, unclear ownership, repeated rework, and abandoned handoffs.

### Capacity

Determine whether ALPHA and BUILD have explicit capacity or depend on overtime and goodwill. Treat permanently planned 100% utilization as evidence of fragility, not efficiency.

### Roles and authority

Separate positions from roles. For critical roles, inspect:

- activities;
- domains of influence;
- authorities and decisions;
- obligations and reporting;
- missing or overlapping ownership.

### Assumptions and evidence

List assumptions behind strategy, pricing, products, hiring, and major programs. Mark each as observed, reported, inferred, contradicted, or unknown. Identify the cheapest valid way to learn.

### Genuine change

Use the `f(x) = ax + b` test: did the intervention change inputs, capabilities, decision rules, behaviors, or outcomes, or did it merely rename roles, rearrange reporting lines, and introduce tools while preserving the same result?

## Common patterns

| Pattern | Signals | Likely constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Operational gravity | Escalations dominate; improvement is repeatedly postponed | RUN stability or design |
| Idea theatre | Many workshops and ideas; few owned experiments | ALPHA-to-BUILD transition |
| Project congestion | Too many initiatives; slow delivery and priority churn | BUILD capacity and governance |
| Cosmetic transformation | New labels and tools; unchanged decisions and outcomes | Causal model and leadership behavior |
| Adviser dependency | External output cannot be evaluated or sustained internally | Knowledge transfer and decision ownership |
| Innovation isolation | Innovation team works separately from operations | Flow into BUILD and RUN |

## Diagnostic sequence

1. Define the decision and scope.
2. Gather a representative activity and initiative inventory.
3. Classify it as RUN, BUILD, ALPHA, or mixed.
4. Map attention and capacity using defensible ranges.
5. Trace the flow of one idea.
6. identify the most binding constraint.
7. Test alternative explanations.
8. Recommend the smallest interventions that address the constraint.

## Evidence standard

Do not turn a rough allocation into a false metric. If information comes from one leader's perception, say so. Recommend a short diary, calendar sample, project review, or interviews when better evidence would change the decision.
