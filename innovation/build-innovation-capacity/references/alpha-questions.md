# ALPHA Questions

## Purpose

Use provocative questions to loosen accepted constraints and expose assumptions. Select two or three that fit the decision. Do not fire the entire list at a team.

## Faithful English question set

- What is so expensive that a competitor would never do it?
- What do our customers not expect from us?
- If we founded the company today, what would we do differently?
- What would become possible if each department had 10–20% free capacity?
- What if we gave away our highest-margin product for free?
- Could we offer outsiders—even competitors—something we currently do only for ourselves?
- What makes up our corporate DNA, and what else could we combine from it?
- What do we believe we know, and how much of it has actually been validated?
- What if competitors attended our most confidential meetings?
- What if the “laziest” person in the company were CEO for a week?
- Regardless of time, people, and money, which three things would move the company forward most?
- What if we stopped the long-awaited giant project and retested all its assumptions?
- After using an external consultancy or agency, are we more capable or more dependent?

## Selection guidance

| Situation | Useful questions |
|---|---|
| Strategy renewal | Founded today; corporate DNA; competitors in the meeting |
| Customer differentiation | Customer does not expect; competitor would never do |
| Operational overload | Free capacity; “laziest” CEO; three wishes |
| Business-model exploration | Give away the margin engine; internal capability offered externally; corporate DNA |
| Major-program review | Retest assumptions; stop the giant project; adviser capability versus dependency |

## Convert a question into work

For each selected question:

1. Capture the surprising answer without immediate rejection.
2. Identify the assumptions that make it appear impossible.
3. Trace the causal chain behind each constraint.
4. Identify who would gain or lose value.
5. formulate a testable hypothesis.
6. choose the smallest ethical and informative experiment.
7. define evidence that would support, weaken, or stop the idea.

## Facilitation safety

Explain that “lazy” refers to finding unnecessary effort, not judging a person's character. Keep challenges focused on systems, assumptions, and choices. Do not use provocation to bypass legal, ethical, financial, safety, or employment obligations.
