## YES, AND

Use before ideation or a difficult change conversation to demonstrate the difference between reflexive rejection and constructive extension.

1. Give the group a low-stakes topic.
2. Have participants respond to an idea with “Yes, but...”.
3. Repeat with “Yes, and...”.
4. Debrief how each mode affected safety, empathy, and possibility.
5. Explain that “Yes, and” may still introduce conditions and improvements; it does not remove critical judgment.

## Role definition

Describe a role through:

- activities;
- domains;
- authorities;
- obligations.

Use role mapping to find missing ownership, repeated approvals, overlaps, process gaps, hiring needs, and onboarding requirements. Keep roles modular rather than treating a position as one indivisible block.

## Corporate DNA Analysis

Use to explore adjacent products and business models.

1. Select relevant elements: physical assets, digital assets, customers, employees and skills, standards, processes, reputation, and patents.
2. For growth exploration, begin with customers, employee skills, reputation, and core processes.
3. Capture each meaningful element on a separate card.
4. Include active, unused, and learnable skills.
5. Recombine cards in a cross-functional group of roughly two to five people.
6. Record promising combinations as concepts.
7. Mark missing capabilities as gaps.
8. Prioritize concepts for customer and feasibility validation.

## Fairy Question

Ask: “Imagine a fairy could grant exactly three wishes connected to your work. Time, people, money, and apparent impossibility do not matter. What would you wish for?”

Use answers from different roles to detect recurring pain, blocked aspirations, and optimization opportunities. Translate wishes into underlying needs before proposing solutions.

## Laziness Check

Use “laziness” carefully as a playful lens for unnecessary effort.

1. Observe recurring, boring, slow, or low-value tasks on three separated days.
2. Record the activity, role, purpose, and reason it feels wasteful.
3. Ask why it exists.
4. Consider stopping, simplifying, removing approvals, standardizing, or automating it.
5. Test changes with the people doing the work.

## Build My Own Job

Explain the organization's vision and objectives, then ask a candidate or employee to describe an ideal role and how it would advance those objectives. Compare the answer with real organizational needs and constraints. Do not imply that every desired role can be created.

## Change-equation test

Represent the current organization as `f(x) = ax + b`. Ask whether a proposed transformation changes the actual function and its output or merely rewrites the same relationship in a more complex form.

Inspect:

- decisions and authority;
- incentives and behavior;
- inputs and capabilities;
- process constraints;
- customer outcomes;
- measurable results.

Use the metaphor to expose cosmetic change, not as a literal quantitative model.
