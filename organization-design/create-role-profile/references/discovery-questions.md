# Discovery Questions

## Interview order

Do not ask every question mechanically. Ask only what is missing and material.

### 1. Purpose and success

Start with:

> Why must this role exist, and what would be observably better if it worked well?

Follow up when needed:

- Which outcomes is the role accountable for?
- Who depends on those outcomes?
- What problem would return if the role disappeared?
- How will the organization recognize success?

### 2. Domains

Ask:

> Which teams, processes, assets, products, standards, budgets, or data fall within this role's scope?

Clarify ownership versus influence, and name boundaries.

### 3. Responsibilities

Ask:

> What recurring activities or outcomes must this role personally ensure?

For each answer, ask whether it is accountable ownership, execution, advice, or support. Split combined statements.

### 4. Decision rights

For each responsibility, ask:

- What information may the role request?
- Which decisions may it make without further approval?
- Which roles may it direct on professional matters?
- Which policies or standards may it create or change?
- What may it inspect and evaluate?
- In which decisions does it advise rather than decide?

Ask for monetary, personnel, scope, quality, or risk thresholds where appropriate.

### 5. Obligations

Ask:

- Which quality or compliance level must the role ensure?
- What must it report, to whom, and when?
- Which actions require another role's approval?

### 6. Interfaces and boundaries

Ask:

- Which roles provide inputs or receive outputs?
- Where are handoffs currently unclear?
- What must this role explicitly not own?
- Which neighboring role owns each excluded area?
- Where could authority overlap or conflict?

### 7. Requirements

Ask:

- Which capabilities are essential on day one?
- Which can be learned?
- Which certifications or experience are truly required, and why?
- Are any listed traits merely preferences for the current holder?

### 8. Governance

Ask:

- Who holds the role now?
- Who approves the role definition?
- When should it be reviewed?
- What organizational change could make it obsolete?

## Fast path

When time is limited, ask five questions:

1. Why does the role exist?
2. Which outcomes and domains does it own?
3. What are its five to ten main responsibilities?
4. What may it decide independently, and what requires approval?
5. What is explicitly outside its scope?
