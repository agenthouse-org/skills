# Role Model

## Core distinction

A **position** usually bundles what one employee does and may do. A **role** is a smaller, reusable unit of organizational accountability. A person can hold multiple roles, and a role can move between people without redesigning an entire position.

## Role components

### Role name

Use a concise, recognizable name that describes the organizational contribution rather than the current holder.

### Purpose

State why the role exists and which outcomes it enables. Prefer two or three sentences. Do not list every task here.

### Domains

Name the assets, processes, products, standards, teams, budgets, data, or other bounded scopes over which the role has influence. Examples: “Product image quality standard,” “Assigned customer accounts,” or “Release calendar.”

### Requirements

Separate:

- required knowledge and skills;
- required experience or certifications when genuinely necessary;
- useful but optional capabilities.

Avoid traits that cannot be assessed or that reproduce bias.

### Responsibilities

Each responsibility should describe an accountable activity or outcome. Begin with a verb when practical. Split unrelated responsibilities into separate rows because their authority and obligations may differ.

## Authority types

| Authority | Meaning | Clarify |
|---|---|---|
| Information | Request and receive information relevant to the responsibility | Sources, data sensitivity, access limits |
| Decision | Decide or approve within the responsibility | Threshold, budget, scope, escalation |
| Functional direction | Direct named roles on professional or process matters | Recipient roles; not automatically disciplinary authority |
| Policy | Create, maintain, or change rules and standards | Scope and required approvals |
| Control | Inspect and evaluate quality or compliance | Standard, evidence, consequence |
| Participation | Advise or contribute to another role's decision | Decision owner and required consultation point |

An empty authority cell means no authority has been established. It must not be read as implicit permission.

## Obligation types

| Obligation | Meaning | Clarify |
|---|---|---|
| Quality control | Ensure a defined quality or compliance level | Standard, measure, corrective action |
| Reporting | Provide status or a defined report | Recipient, cadence, format, trigger |
| Approval | Obtain approval from another role before acting | Approver, threshold, evidence |

## Notes and interfaces

Use notes to name counterpart roles, thresholds, policy references, handoffs, and exceptions. Avoid hiding an essential responsibility or authority only in a note.

## Boundaries and exclusions

State what might reasonably be assumed to belong to the role but does not. Name the actual owning role when known. Boundaries prevent duplicate ownership and title-based assumptions.

## Design tests

A strong role profile answers:

- Why does the role exist?
- What bounded domains does it affect?
- What is the role accountable for?
- What may it decide, direct, inspect, or shape?
- What must it report, control, or seek approval for?
- Where does its authority stop?
- With which roles must it coordinate?
