# Competitive Assessment

## Activation

Use this reference when:

- a competitor is named;
- an incumbent exists;
- the buyer is comparing alternatives;
- a tender or RFP is involved;
- the status quo is a meaningful option.

## Competition Types

Assess:

- named direct competitor;
- incumbent supplier;
- internal build;
- manual process;
- delay or no decision;
- alternative budget priority;
- adjacent category solution.

The strongest competitor is often the status quo.

## Evidence

For each relevant alternative, identify:

- evidence that it is genuinely being considered;
- buyer perception;
- likely strengths;
- likely weaknesses;
- switching costs;
- commercial position;
- relationship advantage;
- specification advantage;
- proof required to differentiate.

Do not present speculation as competitor intelligence.

## Competitive Position

Classify:

- **Advantaged**
- **Competitive**
- **Unclear**
- **Disadvantaged**
- **Structurally unlikely to win**

Explain the classification.

## Differentiation Test

A differentiator should be:

- relevant to the buyer's decision;
- credible;
- provable;
- difficult to neutralize;
- commercially meaningful.

Generic claims such as "better service", "innovative", or "AI-powered" are not sufficient without evidence.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent competitor weaknesses.
- Do not use confidential information improperly.
- Do not make defamatory or unverifiable claims.
- Do not advise price coordination or anti-competitive behavior.
- Treat lack of competitor knowledge as uncertainty, not as absence of competition.
