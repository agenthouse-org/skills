# Probability Model

## Purpose

Probability to close is not the same as qualification score.

- **Qualification score** measures how well the case meets criteria.
- **Probability to close** estimates the chance of winning this specific opportunity.
- **Evidence confidence** measures how reliable the estimate is.
- **Priority** measures whether further investment is worthwhile.

## Step 1: Retrieve a Baseline

Use the best available baseline in this order:

1. company historical win rate for comparable opportunities;
2. company stage-based probability;
3. industry or sales-motion baseline from approved memory;
4. generic conservative baseline.

Do not invent a historical baseline.

## Step 2: Assess Probability Drivers

Assess these drivers:

| Driver | Positive evidence | Negative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | verified, costly, acknowledged | vague, unowned, low impact |
| Value | credible quantified or strongly evidenced value | unclear or marginal value |
| Solution fit | requirements and delivery fit verified | material gaps or over-customization |
| Urgency | compelling event or cost of delay | no timing reason |
| Buying center | champion and key approvers engaged | single weak contact |
| Decision process | documented and realistic | unknown or changing |
| Funding | approved or credible path | no funding path |
| Competition | differentiated, fair access | incumbent lock-in or tailored specification |
| Mutual commitment | buyer invests time, data, access, or action | seller-only activity |
| Execution | feasible implementation and contracting | delivery, legal, or security blockers |

## Step 3: Produce a Range

Prefer a range:

```text
Estimated probability to close: 30–45%
Confidence in estimate: Medium
```

Use a narrow range only when:

- evidence is strong;
- the process is mature;
- historical baselines are reliable;
- major decision variables are known.

Use a wide range when:

- key stakeholders are unknown;
- the decision process is unclear;
- information is contradictory;
- competition is uncertain;
- the estimate relies on inference.

## Step 4: Apply Hard Limits

Probability should generally remain low when:

- no verified problem exists;
- no plausible buying path exists;
- the opportunity is seller-created without buyer commitment;
- a tender was discovered late and the seller lacks differentiation or prior lawful discovery;
- mandatory requirements cannot be met;
- funding is impossible;
- a hard disqualifier applies.

## Confidence Labels

### High

Most material claims are verified, current, and consistent.

### Medium

The core case is credible, but some material elements are buyer-reported, inferred, or incomplete.

### Low

The assessment depends heavily on unknown, seller-reported, stale, or contradictory information.

## Commercial Value

When numbers are available:

```text
Expected contribution =
probability midpoint × expected contribution margin
```

```text
Net pursuit value =
expected contribution
+ strategic value adjustment
− remaining cost of sale
− opportunity cost
− risk reserve
```

When reliable numbers are unavailable, use qualitative bands and state the missing inputs.

## Avoid False Precision

Do not report values such as 37.4% unless a validated statistical model supports that precision.

Do not increase probability merely because:

- a proposal was sent;
- a meeting was held;
- the prospect was polite;
- a senior person attended;
- the CRM stage was manually advanced.
