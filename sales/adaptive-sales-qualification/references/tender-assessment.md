# Tender and Formal Procurement Assessment

## Activation

Use this reference when the opportunity involves:

- a public tender;
- an RFP or RFQ;
- a formal bid;
- a framework agreement;
- a procurement portal;
- a competitive request with fixed requirements or deadlines.

## Core Questions

### Procurement Context

- What type of procedure is involved?
- What jurisdiction and rules apply?
- What are the mandatory requirements?
- What are the award criteria and weightings?
- What is the response deadline?
- What clarification process is permitted?
- What evidence, certifications, or references are required?

### Opportunity Origin

Classify the seller's position:

- **Reactive participant** — discovered after publication with little prior context;
- **Informed participant** — had lawful prior discovery or customer understanding;
- **Incumbent** — has existing delivery knowledge or relationship;
- **Market-consultation participant** — joined a documented lawful pre-market process;
- **Unknown position** — evidence is insufficient.

Lawful prior understanding can improve relevance and preparation. It must never be confused with improper influence.

### Commercial Position

- Does the seller understand the underlying problem or only the written specification?
- Does the specification appear neutral, incumbent-oriented, or unusually tailored?
- Can all mandatory requirements be met?
- Is there a credible differentiator against the likely field?
- Is price dominant?
- Is the contract commercially acceptable?
- Is the implementation timeline feasible?
- Is bid effort proportionate to win probability and expected contribution?

### Buyer Access

- Is clarification access equal and formally allowed?
- Was any prior engagement lawful and documented?
- Are communications compliant with procurement rules?
- Is there material information asymmetry?
- Are there conflicts of interest?

## Tender Position Signals

### Positive

- early lawful understanding of the customer problem;
- clear fit with mandatory and weighted criteria;
- credible differentiation;
- relevant references;
- feasible timeline;
- documented budget;
- balanced contract;
- acceptable bid cost;
- transparent clarification channel.

### Negative

- discovered very late;
- no understanding beyond published documents;
- specification strongly matches an incumbent;
- mandatory gaps;
- unrealistic implementation;
- price-only competition without cost advantage;
- disproportionate bid effort;
- unclear evaluation criteria;
- unacceptable liability or payment terms;
- suspected procedural irregularity.

## No-Bid Triggers

Recommend no-bid when:

- a mandatory requirement cannot be met;
- the response would require false or misleading claims;
- procurement rules cannot be followed;
- bid cost is uneconomic relative to realistic expected value;
- the timeline prevents a credible response;
- contract risk is unacceptable;
- evidence suggests the seller is merely being added to create formal competition;
- the seller has no meaningful differentiation and faces a strongly advantaged incumbent.

## Guardrail

Never recommend:

- secretly influencing requirements;
- obtaining privileged information;
- bypassing formal clarification channels;
- coordinating bids;
- misrepresenting references, certifications, or capabilities;
- exploiting personal relationships to violate procurement rules.

The skill may acknowledge commercial reality while remaining within lawful and ethical boundaries.
